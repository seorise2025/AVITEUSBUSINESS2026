Running payroll for a small business: the employer checklist

In short

Each pay run, you calculate pay, tax and National Insurance, report it to HMRC through RTI on or before payday, and give staff payslips. Each month, you pay PAYE to HMRC by the 22nd if you pay electronically. Each year, staff get a P60 by 31 May and any P11D forms go in by 6 July. Workplace pension duties run alongside all of it.

Before your first payday

  • Register as an employer with HMRC. You can do this up to 2 months before you first pay anyone. You get a PAYE reference and an Accounts Office reference.
  • Check each person's right to work in the UK before they start.
  • Get employers' liability insurance. Most employers must have it from the first day.
  • Set up workplace pension duties. Your duties start from the first day you employ someone. You must confirm what you have done to The Pensions Regulator (the declaration of compliance).

Every pay run

  • Collect changes: starters, leavers, hours, overtime, bonuses and sickness.
  • Calculate pay, tax, National Insurance, pension and any statutory pay.
  • Send a Full Payment Submission (FPS) to HMRC on or before payday. This is Real Time Information, or RTI.
  • Give every employee a payslip.
  • Pay your staff.

Every month

TaskDeadline
Pay PAYE and National Insurance to HMRC22nd of the month if paying electronically (19th by post)

Pension contributions to your providerBy the date your provider and the rules set

Small employers often pay HMRC quarterly instead of monthly. Check with HMRC whether you qualify before you change.

Every year

  • Last payday of the tax year: mark that pay run's FPS as the final one for the year.
  • By 31 May: give every employee their P60.
  • By 6 July: send P11D and P11D(b) forms if you provided benefits in kind.
  • Every three years: re-enrol staff into your workplace pension and confirm it to The Pensions Regulator.

The deadline finder lists your next payroll dates in order.

Common payroll mistakes

  • Late RTI. The report must reach HMRC on or before payday, not after.
  • Wrong tax codes left unchanged after HMRC sends an update.
  • Forgetting a leaver's P45, or paying someone after they have left without reporting it.
  • Missing pension deadlines, which The Pensions Regulator can fine for.

Doing it yourself, or handing it over

If you pay one director a fixed salary and nothing changes, payroll software may be all you need. Once you have staff with hours, sickness and starters, a person running it to a fixed monthly routine saves you the chase. See how our managed payroll month works.

Questions

When must RTI be sent to HMRC?

On or before each payday. Each pay run needs its own Full Payment Submission.

When is PAYE due?

By the 22nd of the month if you pay electronically, for the tax month that ended on the 5th. By post, it is the 19th.

When do employees get their P60?

By 31 May, for the tax year that ended on 5 April.
