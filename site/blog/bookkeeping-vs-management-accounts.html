Bookkeeping vs management accounts: what is the difference?

In short

Bookkeeping is recording and matching every transaction. Management accounts are the short monthly report built from those records: profit, cash and what changed. Statutory accounts are the yearly accounts the law requires. You need up-to-date books before management accounts are worth reading.

What bookkeeping covers

  • Every bank and card line matched to a bill, a sale or a receipt.
  • Supplier bills and sales invoices recorded and coded for VAT.
  • A list of what is missing, such as receipts for card payments.
  • The month closed, so the figures stop moving.

Good bookkeeping is invisible. You notice it only when it is missing, usually a week before a VAT return.

What management accounts contain

  • Profit and loss for the month and the year to date.
  • Balance sheet: what the business owns and owes.
  • Cash position and what is coming in and going out.
  • Notes in plain English on what moved and why. This is the part most owners actually read.

When management accounts are worth it

You probably need them if any of these is true:

  • You employ staff and want to know if you can afford the next hire.
  • You borrow money, or plan to. Lenders often ask for recent management accounts, and a forecast helps too.
  • Your margins move from month to month and you do not know why.
  • You only find out if you made money when the yearly accounts arrive.

If you have very few transactions and no staff, good software and a quarterly check may be enough.

What good management accounts look like

Short, on time and readable. Three pages is plenty. They should arrive at the same point each month, and the notes should answer "why" in a sentence or two. See how our monthly bookkeeping and management pack work.

Questions

Can I have management accounts without a bookkeeper?

Only if your books are up to date. Management accounts are built from your records, so if the records are behind, the report will be wrong.

Are management accounts the same as year-end accounts?

No. Year-end (statutory) accounts are a legal filing made once a year. Management accounts are for running the business and are not filed anywhere.

How often should I get management accounts?

Monthly suits most growing businesses. Quarterly can work if your figures do not change much from month to month.
