Making Tax Digital for Income Tax: who it affects and what to do

In short

Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax started on 6 April 2026. It applies to sole traders and landlords whose income from self-employment and property, before expenses, is over £50,000. The limit falls to £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028. If you are in, you keep digital records, send HMRC a short update every quarter, and finish the year with a final declaration by 31 January. Limited companies are not affected.

Who has to use it, and when

Your qualifying incomeBased on your tax return forYou start on
Over £50,0002024 to 20256 April 2026
Over £30,0002025 to 20266 April 2027
Over £20,0002026 to 20276 April 2028

Qualifying income is your total income from self-employment and property, before expenses. It is not your profit. A wage from a job, pension income and dividends do not count. If you own a property jointly, count your share.

Earn £35,000 from a trade and £20,000 in rent? That is £55,000, so you were in from April 2026, even if your profit was much lower.

Who it does not apply to (yet)

  • Limited companies. They pay corporation tax, not Income Tax, so MTD for Income Tax does not apply to the company. Directors who also have rental income may still be in, if that income is over the limit.
  • Partnerships. Not yet. The government has not set a date.
  • Qualifying income under £20,000. You carry on with a normal Self Assessment tax return.
  • People who cannot use digital tools because of age, disability, where they live or another reason. You can ask HMRC for an exemption.

What changes in practice

  1. Digital records. You keep your income and expenses in software that works with MTD, not in a paper book. A spreadsheet can work if it links to bridging software.
  2. Quarterly updates. Every three months, you send HMRC a summary of your income and expenses for each trade and for your property income.
  3. A final declaration. After the tax year ends, you add any other income and reliefs and confirm the final figures, by 31 January. This replaces the old tax return.

When you pay does not change. Tax is still due on 31 January, with payments on account on 31 January and 31 July where they apply.

The quarterly update dates

QuarterUpdate due by
6 April to 5 July7 August
6 July to 5 October7 November
6 October to 5 January7 February
6 January to 5 April7 May

You can choose calendar quarters instead (to 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March). The due dates stay the same. Late updates earn penalty points, in the same way as late VAT returns.

What to do now

  • Already in since April 2026? Your updates for the quarters to 5 July and 5 October should be filed. The next one is due by 7 February 2027.
  • Income over £30,000 in 2025 to 2026? You start on 6 April 2027. Choose your software now and run it for a few months first, so April is not your first try.
  • Not sure of your figure? Add up your trading and rental income before expenses from your last tax return.
  • Pick software that is ready. Xero, QuickBooks, Sage and FreeAgent all have versions for MTD for Income Tax. We set up the software and train you.

Questions

When did Making Tax Digital for Income Tax start?

On 6 April 2026, for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000.

Does MTD for Income Tax apply to limited companies?

No. Companies pay corporation tax, so MTD for Income Tax does not apply to them. A director with rental income over the limit may still need to use it for that income.

Do I still need to file a Self Assessment tax return?

If you are in MTD for Income Tax, the final declaration by 31 January replaces the tax return. If your qualifying income is under the limit, you file a normal Self Assessment return.

Is qualifying income my profit?

No. It is your total income from self-employment and property before you take off expenses.

Can I use a spreadsheet?

Only with bridging software that sends the figures to HMRC. Most people find accounting software simpler.
