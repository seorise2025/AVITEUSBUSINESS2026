Switching

How to change accountant in the UK: a step-by-step guide

In short

You can change accountant at any time of year. Your new accountant does most of the work: they ask your old firm for professional clearance, collect your records and get authority to deal with HMRC for you. Your part is usually one email and a few approvals. The real risk is timing, so plan the move around your next payroll run and VAT return.

Before you start: read your engagement letter

Your current accountant should have given you an engagement letter. It sets out the notice period and how fees are settled when you leave. Check two things:

  • Notice period. Many firms ask for 30 days. Some ask for less.
  • Fees owed. Clear any unpaid invoices, or agree how they will be paid. Unpaid fees are the most common reason a handover slows down.

You do not need a reason to leave. You also do not need your current firm's permission.

Step 1: Agree the new scope in writing

Before you tell anyone, get a written scope from the new firm. It should say what they will do, what you will do, when, and the fixed fee. Our scope table for monthly bookkeeping shows what that looks like.

Ask the new firm one question: which date will you take over from? A clean answer is "from the start of your next VAT quarter" or "from your next pay run".

Step 2: Tell your current accountant

A short email is enough. Keep it polite and factual:

Hello [name],

We have decided to move our accounting work to another firm from [date]. They will be in touch for professional clearance and the handover. Please let me know if any fees are outstanding.

Thank you for your help.

Step 3: Professional clearance

Your new accountant writes to your old one to ask if there is any professional reason they should not take on the work. This is called professional clearance. Accountants who belong to a professional body, such as ICAEW or ACCA, follow this step as part of their ethics rules.

The same letter asks for the information needed to carry on the work, such as the last accounts and tax returns, and any open matters with HMRC.

Step 4: Authority to talk to HMRC

Your new accountant asks HMRC to become your agent for each tax they will handle: corporation tax, VAT, PAYE and Self Assessment. You then approve each request. Until this is done, HMRC will not discuss your affairs with them.

Tip: approve these quickly. A missing authority is the usual reason a VAT return or payroll submission is held up during a switch.

When is the best time to switch?

Any time works, but some moments are easier:

  • Just after a VAT return is filed. The new firm starts with a clean quarter.
  • Just after a pay run. Payroll moves from the next run, with no gap. Our payroll page explains how moving payroll part way through the year works.
  • Not in the last weeks before a big deadline. If your accounts or tax return are due within a month, let the old firm finish that one.

Questions

Do I need to tell HMRC that I have changed accountant?

Not directly. Your new accountant sends HMRC the requests to act for you, and you approve them. That is enough for HMRC.

Can I change accountant part way through the year?

Yes. There is no fixed time to switch. The new firm picks up from the last month that was completed and checks the opening figures first.

Will I lose my accounting records?

No, as long as the accounting software is in your company's name. If the old firm holds the subscription, ask them to transfer it to you before you leave.

How long does a switch take?

Usually a few weeks, most of it waiting for clearance and HMRC approvals. Planning around your next payroll and VAT dates means nothing is missed while that happens.
